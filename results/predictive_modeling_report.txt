────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHY LOO-CV?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
With only n=16 states, traditional train/test splits lose too much data.
LOO-CV trains on 15 states, predicts the 16th, and rotates through all
16 combinations. Every state gets predicted exactly once, without ever
being in its own training set. This is the gold standard for small-n.

KEY INSIGHTS FOR THE PAPER
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. STATISTICAL ↔ ML CONVERGENCE:
   The OLS regression (R²=0.610) and ML models produce consistent results,
   validating that the Digital Access → CE Behavior relationship is not an
   artifact of a single statistical method.

2. POLICY IMPLICATION:
   If ML models can predict CE behavior from digital indicators,
   policymakers can use readily-available digital metrics (tele-density,
   UPI adoption) as early predictors of a state's circular economy readiness.

3. REGULARIZED MODELS:
   Ridge and Lasso regression outperforming basic OLS suggests that
   regularization helps with the small sample size, preventing overfitting
   to individual state outliers.

4. NON-LINEAR MODELS:
   Random Forest and Gradient Boosting results indicate whether the
   Digital-CE relationship has non-linear components beyond what OLS captures.
