Digital inclusion and circular economy policies share an interest in information access and service coordination, but their empirical association depends on how development indicators are constructed. This exploratory study combines seven traceable public input tables for 22 Indian states, covering telecommunications, digital payments, income, sustainability, formal e-waste processing, and projected population characteristics. A digital access score and a circularity-related proxy are constructed without shared income components. State resampling, income-adjusted regression, and nested predictive evaluation distinguish broad sustainability performance from formal material-processing activity. The index association is positive (Pearson r=0.779; Spearman rho=0.810), but the digital-access coefficient is uncertain after income adjustment (robust p=0.162). Digital-only ElasticNet achieves pooled outer leave-one-state-out R-squared of 0.658 for the Sustainable Development Goals composite and 0.292 for log e-waste processing intensity. Extreme gradient boosting does not improve this comparison. The findings support a descriptive connection with broader development capacity while providing more limited evidence for formal circular material processing. Mixed reference years, state-level aggregation, rounded secondary payment estimates, projected denominators, missing-state selection, and the small sample prevent causal or prospective forecasting claims. Environmental monitoring should distinguish broad sustainability indicators from direct evidence of collection, repair, reuse, and material recovery.
