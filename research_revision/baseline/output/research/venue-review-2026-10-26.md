**Substantive changes needed before any submission.** These observations come from the supplied project, not assumptions about its authors.

1. **The described nested tuning is not implemented correctly.** [manuscript methodology](</Users/yashchauhan/Desktop/minor project/Minor_project_overleaf/main.tex:226>) and `:421` describe an inner four-fold loop. [nested tuning implementation](</Users/yashchauhan/Desktop/minor project/src/06_advanced_ml.py:238>) instead uses `LeaveOneOut()` with `GridSearchCV(..., scoring='r2')` at line 241. Every inner test fold has one observation. The [official scikit-learn documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.r2_score.html) states that R² is undefined with fewer than two samples. The tuning scores therefore cannot support the described hyperparameter selection. Rerun using a valid loss such as negative MSE inside LOO, or appropriate multi-observation inner folds. Recompute all dependent tables and claims. The reported nested XGBoost R² of 0.511 is not validated by this audit.

2. **The normalization equation and saved results disagree.** The manuscript says `(x - min)/(max - min) × 100`. The implementation at [DAS implementation](</Users/yashchauhan/Desktop/minor project/src/01_data_engineering.py:165>) and `:174` uses `x/max × 100`, with SDG scores used directly. I independently reconstructed the latter formulas: after rounding, they match every saved DAS and CEBS value. Applying the printed min–max formula to the normalized components instead changes the main correlation to approximately **0.812**, rather than **0.781**. Choose, justify, and consistently document one method.

3. **Income is shared by both the explanatory index and outcome.** DAS contains 30% normalized NSDP, and CEBS contains 25%. The full ML feature set also includes NSDP. This creates mathematical overlap and makes income's ability to predict CEBS partly built into the definition. Holding out states does not remove this construct problem. In a diagnostic calculation using the saved data, removing NSDP from both indices and rescaling remaining weights changes Pearson r from **0.781 to about 0.478**. Using the digital-only composite against e-waste alone gives about **0.259**. These are exploratory sensitivity checks, not replacement headline results; they show why stronger independence and construct validation are needed.

4. **“Circular economy behavior” is stronger than the measurements establish.** Overall SDG score and income are broad development proxies. E-waste processing can reflect recycler location, industrial capacity, or inter-state shipments rather than residents' behavior. Use a more precise label, validate against direct circularity measures, and explain the construct limitations. A 21-state analysis that removes e-waste changes the outcome itself; it is not an independent replication of the same CE measure.

5. **Some raw inputs needed for replication are missing or referenced at the wrong path.** `data/raw/` contains only the TRAI, RBI, and NITI CSVs. `dataful_upi_percapita_2024_25.csv`, required at line 57 of the engineering script, is absent. The CPCB and NPCI Excel files are at project root while the script expects them under `data/`. Restore the source material and document exact source tables, years, state boundaries, units, population denominators, and dependency versions. Do not describe the current checkout as a complete independently verified replication package.

6. **A directly relevant published DCI comparator is missing from the manuscript.** Giraldi, Rossi, and Olivieri's *Digital platforms and composite measurement of an urban circular economy: Developing the digital circularity index*, Technology in Society 86 (2026), 103322, was available online **24 March 2026**. It studies **14 Italian metropolitan cities** using PLS path modelling. Your work concerns Indian states and uses different indicators and methods, so this does not by itself invalidate your contribution. It does require citation, comparison, and a narrower novelty claim. “Digital Circularity Index” alone is not an original name at today's submission date. [Publisher article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160791X26001119); [author-institution full text](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/handle/2318/2131750/2020227/2026_Digital%20platforms%20and%20composite%20measurement%20urban%20circular%20economy.pdf).

7. **Interpret small-sample results more cautiously.** Sixteen observations, multiple models, a data-selected threshold supported by very few states, and a highly influential Haryana observation do not establish a robust forecasting system or a causal policy effect. SHAP explanations fitted on all 16 states explain that fitted model; they do not identify causal drivers. A nonsignificant mediation test does not establish that UPI cannot be a mechanism. Describe clustering as exploratory; repeated four-fold results are variable in the saved report.

8. **Correct internal and figure reporting.** The manuscript calls the digital-only model a three-variable model, but [digital feature list](</Users/yashchauhan/Desktop/minor project/src/05_predictive_modeling.py:107>) supplies two features: tele-density and UPI. The Shapiro–Wilk row reports skewness instead of the test's W statistic and p-value. Durbin–Watson on an arbitrary state ordering does not establish absence of spatial dependence. Figure 5's caption says left/right, but the two panels are vertically stacked, and Figure 6 calls its profile chart a radar chart although it is a grouped bar chart. Several figure labels will be hard to read at final column width.

The original bivariate arithmetic does check out on the saved CSV: slope **0.55148**, intercept **11.15829**, Pearson r **0.78129**, and R² **0.61041**. DAS mean **64.9519**, CEBS mean **46.9781**, and DCI range **8.24–72.97** also agree. Agreement with a saved table does not independently validate the underlying sources, construct, or nested-CV claim.
